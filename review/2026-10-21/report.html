eAMF Code Sweep

Sixteen review agents read the live pricing code one area at a time, looking for bugs and methodology problems. Each tested what it suspected with short scripts, on synthetic matches or on the run outputs already in the repo. I then re-checked every high finding against the code and re-ran the key numbers.

Covered: eAMFModel (v2.1, its stream, the trader and the shared modules), eAMFCalibrator, glmer, nb2 and xgb, about 55k lines. Left out: the legacy v14 and sim14 files, which v2.1 no longer imports, and the exploratory analysis/ scripts. Nothing in the repo was changed.

Bottom line

  • HeadlineThe v21-drives result against prod (+3.85 points on the same bets, Oct 12–18) isn't evidence yet that v21 prices better. +3.40 of it comes from the 6% of stake whose spread or total bets flip result at v21's own line. Spread and total bets at the same line add +0.05. See A1.
  • TestsThe same-bets reprice and the default margin uplift both reward any model whose errors differ from prod's, because punters choose where prod is wrong. Neither can tell a better model from a differently wrong one (A2, A3).
  • Priorv2.1's default prior was picked on the same October bets it's then scored on. Its fast rating over-reacts as a forecast (calibration slope 0.39). Its held-out gain on every bet isn't significant (A4).
  • Variantsv21-drives against v21 is +0.12 [−0.46, +0.75], and form against avg is also inside the noise. The pace nudge isn't shown to help or hurt yet (A5).
  • ModelFour model problems stand out. The tilts are shrunk several times harder than documented, most of all on pace. The game ends too early in some late states with the clock stopped. Overtime starts forget who has had the ball. Very unlikely outcomes are quoted at 0.01% (B1–B4).
  • SundaySeveral bet tools turn UTC times into seconds as if they were local time. On a UK-time machine, any run that includes the 25 Oct clock change gets gaps an hour wrong (D2).

Fix first

  1. Re-score v21-drives against prod three ways: line bets at prod's line (odds only), a two-book uplift with --all-moments, and intervals resampled by day. Add a placebo to each, prod plus noise and prod's total line moved a point. A1 A2 A3 A5
  2. Freeze every prior setting (drift, weight, blend, nudge share) on pre-October data, and score against prod only after that. A4 A7 A8
  3. Fix the time conversion in the bet tools before running anything over 25 Oct. D2
  4. In the sim: stopped-clock game endings, overtime possession in the starts, and smoothed tail probabilities. B2 B3 B4
  5. Rescale the tilt penalty and re-tune TILT_PRIOR walk-forward. Then check whether plain v21 spreads drives without the pace nudge. B1
  6. Gate incomplete feed matches out of builds and pricing, and require a time check for "pre-match". C1 C2
  7. Fix or drop run prod|candidate|both, which crashes. D1

A2HighMethodologyVerified

The same-bets reprice credits any model whose errors differ from prod's

Where
bets.py:376 (reprice), book_comparison.py:65 (elasticity 0)
Found
Punters back the spots where prod is generous, and the reprice keeps their side and stake. A candidate with unrelated errors removes prod's mistakes on those bets and is never charged for its own. In a synthetic test, a model twice as noisy as prod scored +4.01 [+3.17, +4.80] on the same bets and −13.25 [−19.19, −7.03] in two books. The effect inflates two other results:

Lead with a two-book comparison on all moments. Show same-bets only beside a placebo (prod plus noise the size of prod's own error) and the "expected if prod were right" term.

A4HighMethodologyVerified

BModel and pricing

Problems that change v2.1's prices.

B1HighMethodologyRe-checked

Tilts are shrunk far harder than "200 snaps", most of all on pace

Where
eAMFModel/v21.py:2078 fit_exp_tilts: lam = prior × var(phi) over all snaps
Found
The penalty uses phi's spread across all snaps, but each snap only carries the spread within its own state. For time, the stopped-versus-running split makes that gap large. In a test with stopped and running states:
  • a gamer with 200 snaps moved 0.13 of the way to their own fit (documented 0.50);
  • a gamer with 1,000 snaps moved 0.41 (documented 0.83).
On a fake build the effective prior is about 500 snaps for time. This fits the README's note that v21's spread barely moves with a match's pace. The size on the real build hasn't been measured.
Fix
Scale the penalty by the mean within-state variance (the average Hessian at theta 0). Then re-tune TILT_PRIOR walk-forward.
B2MediumBugTwo reviewers · code checked

The game ends early in late states with the clock stopped

Where
v21.py:640 auto_end, used in simulate
Found
The rule assumes 40 seconds run off before every remaining down. It never checks whether the clock is stopped, and it ignores the two-minute warning.
  • Leader on 1st and 10 at 2:20 just after a kick-off, defense out of timeouts: the sim ends the game at once. Really, the leader snaps at 2:20, 2:00, 1:18 and 0:36, then faces 4th down with about 30 seconds left.
  • 4th and 6 at 0:35 after an incompletion: the game ended in 200 of 200 paths.
In those states the leader's moneyline is too high and the Under too likely.
Fix
Pass whether the clock is running. With it stopped, use (4 − down) × 40 plus kneel time, and allow for the warning above 2:00 in Q4.
B3MediumBugTwo reviewers · code checked

Overtime starts forget who has had the ball

Where
v21.py:2848 (set from the score only), match_starts, v21_stream.state_starts, trader.start_of
Found
At a level overtime after one possession, the sim starts as if overtime were fresh. Take OT at 7-7, home has punted, away has the ball: home wins 0.489 as built, but 0.349 with home's possession marked done. That 14-point gap reaches both the trader and the betting sim's in-play overtime books.
Fix
Carry each side's had-the-ball flag into the start: from the feed's overtime rows in the stream, and from game.ot_had_ball in the trader.
B4MediumMethodologyVerified

Very unlikely outcomes are quoted at 0.01%

Where
eAMFModel/stream.py:255 _quote, 1,000 games a price
Found
On fake matches, 6.2% of moneyline quotes sat at exactly 0.01% or 99.99%. A true 0.3% event comes up 0 of 1,000 about 5% of the time.
  • When such an event happens it costs 9.2 nats of log loss. Sim noise alone then pushes the calibration slope below 1.
  • In the reprice, a longshot bet gets up to 100× prod's odds.
Fix
Smooth the shares, e.g. (k + 0.5)/(n + 1), and floor quotes around 0.5%. Or play more games in decided states.
B5MediumMethodologyVerified

A buzzer 4th down with no result row is kept only if the kick was good

Where
v21.py:1364 decision_points
Found
Missed kicks, go-for-its and run-outs are dropped, so the half's last-seconds cells are all made field goals. The build log counts 305 such missing rows. This leans the sim toward kicking when 3 behind late, which means overtime. The sim's overtime rate is already too high: 3.9% against 2.1% real.
Fix
Treat these rows the same way whatever the score did: drop them all, or record a no-score as "go".
B6MediumMethodologyVerified

The react layer treats a nudge unit as one point on one side

Where
v21_react.py:337, v21_react_fit.py:273; the unit is set at v21.py:1889
Found
The unit is measured with both sides nudged alike. Nudging one side moves its own points 1.44× and the other side's −0.50×, and the real build prints 1.57 points a unit. As a result:
  • the pts and wpa variants move prices 1.4–1.8× further than intended;
  • react-fit's slopes are measured on the wrong scale;
  • g-variants apply gamer scales fitted at another setting without a check (gsnap8 hits the nudge cap 21% of the time).
This affects the experimental react variants only, not v21-drives.
Fix
Measure an own/cross response matrix and convert points to units with it. Refuse a g-variant whose T differs from the fitted setting.

Checked and sound

  • v21_t10 was fitted on matches before 1 Oct. The cut uses the match code's date, which equals the UTC start date on every row.
  • The priors are built walk-forward:
    • NB2 and glmer are refitted daily;
    • the pace prior and cadence.R wait 36 minutes for a result;
    • the layers are fitted on earlier days.
  • The production pace prior is calibrated on the sim's own drive count (r 0.30, slope 1.01, 1 Sep–18 Oct). Its ridge reproduces the README's r 0.279 and slope 1.02.
  • The market maths holds up:
    • home and away, and over and under, add to 100 in every state under all five line modes;
    • pushes are handled right;
    • market 53's sign is right.
  • Home/away and TEAM_A/TEAM_B are mapped correctly in the build, the starts, the trader and the bets.
  • glmer's R code has no problems: features come strictly from before each row, and the pricing and refit windows are correct.
  • The xgb models are built properly:
    • features come only from the past;
    • early stopping uses training data only;
    • the REML fit matches the textbook.

How this was done

Each of the 16 agents took one area and read its code and the matching README sections. They tested suspicions with short scripts, on synthetic matches from eAMFModel/tests/fakes.py or on the repo's own outputs: the out_bets_* bet files, the walkthrough priors and the AMFELO history. Where several reviewers found the same thing, it's merged into one finding. I re-checked each high finding in the code, and re-ran the same-bets split and the shrinkage test.

The tags on each finding:
  • Verified: a run showed the problem.
  • Re-checked: I ran it again myself.
  • Code checked: I confirmed it by reading the code.
Severity levels:
  • High: changes a price or a headline result materially, or crashes a normal run.
  • Medium: wrong in a realistic but narrower case, or biases a result modestly.
  • Low: an edge case.
